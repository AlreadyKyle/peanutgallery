# Mission and vision

## Mission

Make game development a show anyone can watch, steer and fund.

## Vision

The most watched game studio in the world, where a crowd and a team of AI agents build games together in public.

## Promise

"Watch AI agents build a game studio and free games." This is the pitch on every public surface (`docs/PLAN.md` §10 decision 58), and it does not change with the final state.

## What the studio is, in one paragraph

Mob Machine is a game studio whose staff are AI agents and whose producers are its audience. Anyone can play its games free in a browser. Anyone can suggest what it builds next. Supporters fund cards, each a small, concrete change with a funding bar; when a bar fills, the agents build the change, automated checks test it, the Directors review it, and it goes live while everyone watches. The studio itself is drawn as a live room where the agents can be seen at work, and it grows through eras as the crowd's money and the studio's capabilities grow. The ledger shows every dollar in and every dollar spent.

## What makes it different

| Most studios | Mob Machine |
|---|---|
| Development is hidden until launch | Development is the product: every card's steps, checks, failures and fixes are public |
| Players wait for a roadmap they cannot change | Players suggest, fund and watch changes ship, often the same day |
| Crowdfunding sells a promise of a finished game | A card is funded only when it is ready and built when it is funded; nothing is pre-sold |
| Funding pays salaries that nobody can see | The ledger shows what each card cost in model time and where the rest went |
| Collectibles are bought and gambled for | Card rarity is a published function anyone can check; nobody pays for a roll |
| AI is either hidden or the whole pitch | AI agents are the cast, drawn as characters; the crowd and the board steer them, and hard rules bind them |

## Principles we will not trade

Each principle is a test for any feature or card. A proposal that breaks one is refused, however well it would sell.

1. **Spectacle first, and it must be real.** The studio is fun to watch because what happens is real: real money, real agents, real failures. Nothing on the site is staged, scripted or animated to look busier than it is. The live studio draws only what the data says.
2. **Money buys changes for everyone.** A supporter funds a change that every player gets. Nobody buys an advantage, a vote weight, a better rarity or a place in line.
3. **No selling the future.** A card is funded when it meets the definition of ready and built when funded. A goal milestone names only an outcome the studio will deliver from money already in hand. New games are funded in stages, and a stage that misses its bar does not start.
4. **Excitement without gambling.** Rarity, XP, badges and collections give the thrill of a pack opening with none of its money mechanics: rarity is a pure function of the card id with published odds, contributing rolls nothing, and XP cannot be bought, sold or spent.
5. **The crowd's words never reach a writing agent.** Public text is read by roles with no write tools, which return only typed fields (`docs/PLAN.md` §4 Kernel). Hive coding here means the crowd proposes, the agents prepare and fixed rules decide what ships.
6. **All ages, everywhere.** Every string, picture and game meets the all-ages rating. Supporters and players may be minors, so there are no referral payouts and no chance cues near money.
7. **Open books.** The ledger is the record. The feed tells it as a story, and the story never contradicts the record.
8. **Quality is a product feature.** A game that looks like a prototype undercuts the whole show. Every game meets `game-quality.md` before it is called shipped, and the site meets its design bar (`platform/site/DESIGN.md`).
9. **Progress moves on dependencies, money and quality.** No artificial daily limits, timers or streaks. Work starts when what it depends on is done, the money is there and the quality bar is met.
10. **The board is a safety, not a bottleneck.** The studio never waits on the board (`docs/PLAN.md` §10 decisions 66 and 74). The board keeps the duties that need a human or legal identity, and the kill switch.

## What the studio is not

- Not a token, coin or crypto project. The coin on the site is a symbol for US dollars.
- Not a gambling product. No paid randomness, no loot boxes, no odds that money can change.
- Not a game engine or a no-code tool. The games are made by the studio's agents for everyone to play.
- Not a promise of a big game later. Each game ships small and grows card by card.

## How we will know it is working

The mission is measured by three numbers, each defined in `benchmarks.md`:

1. **Watchers.** People who watch the studio in a month: site visitors who stay on the studio, and stream viewers.
2. **Steerers.** People who steered it in a month: suggestions accepted, upvotes, seeds published and contributions.
3. **Shipped changes.** Cards that went live in a month, and how many of them a player can see.

The vision is reached when the studio is among the most watched game-development channels on Twitch and its own site, and the crowd funds more work than the agents can build without the capacity rising.
